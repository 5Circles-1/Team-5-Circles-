The real thing

Put it on the internet — about 10 minutes, free

Do this once, on a computer. You’ll paste two files into Google and get back a web address that belongs to your team. After that, everyone just opens the link on their phone. No accounts to create, no card, nothing to install — it runs on the Google account you already have.

1Open a new Google script

Go to script.new, signed in as webinars5circles@gmail.com. A page called Untitled project opens with a file named Code.gs.

Click Untitled project at the top and name it 5C Pulse.

If script.new doesn’t open, go to script.google.com and press New project.

2Paste file 1 into Code.gs

Click inside the editor, select everything that’s already there (Ctrl + A), and paste file 1 over it.

If the copy button doesn’t work, open this and copy it yourself

3Add a second file named index

In the Files list on the left, click the + and choose HTML. Name it exactly index — Google adds the “.html” by itself.

Open it, select everything (Ctrl + A), and paste file 2 over it. Then save everything with Ctrl + S.

4Put it online

Top right: Deploy›New deployment. Next to “Select type”, press the gear and choose Web app. Then set:

Execute as: Me  ·  Who has access: Anyone

Press Deploy. Google will ask you to Authorize access — pick your account.

You’ll see a warning that Google hasn’t verified this app. That’s normal — it means “this is your own private script, not an app from the store”. Click Advanced, then Go to 5C Pulse (unsafe), then Allow.

“Who has access: Anyone” only means the login screen is reachable — every screen beyond it needs a PIN.

5That address is your team’s tool

Google shows a Web app URL ending in /exec. Copy it and keep it — that link is 5C Pulse for your whole team, on every device.

Open it. You’ll get the welcome screen you just tried in the demo: type the company name and your own name, and the tool makes you the admin and hands you your PIN.

Write your PIN down the moment you see it. It’s shown once. (If you ever lose it, any other admin can reset it — so make a second admin once the team is in.)

6Invite the team from your phone, one name at a time

In the app: Team›Add someone. Each person gets their own PIN, shown to you once, with a Copy invite message button — it writes the WhatsApp message for you: the link, their face, their PIN.

They open the link on their phone, tap their face, type the PIN — that’s the whole training.

Good to know

Questions you’ll have on day two

Where exactly is our data?

In a spreadsheet called “5C Pulse — Team Data” in the Google Drive of the account that did the setup. The Team tab has an Open the Google Sheet button. Every person, task, daily update, message and answer is a row there — it’s your permanent record, and you can read it like any sheet.

We already have a CRM — why this?

The CRM knows your customers. This knows your team. Every evening each person spends one minute on their own questions — the video editor on videos delivered, the marketer on leads and spend, sales on calls and closures, the mentor on sessions, the front desk on walk-ins — plus how the day felt and anything they want to say. You open the Updates tab and read the whole company’s day in one scroll, with streaks and weekly totals per person. No role is hard-coded: the starter question sets are yours to reword in the Team tab, and a brand-new kind of work gets its own set in a minute — so the tool fits whatever team you run next.

Someone keeps “forgetting” their update.

The Updates tab shows who’s still to come. Tap the 🔔 next to their name (or Ring them all) and their screen takes over and keeps ringing until they answer — the same bell as everything else, on record like everything else. And in the Team tab you can set an evening reminder: every day at your chosen time, whoever hasn’t sent that day’s update gets an email — it runs on Google’s side even when nobody has the app open.

How does the bell work? What if their tab is closed?

A ring takes over the other person’s screen and keeps sounding until they pick an answer — and their answer comes back to you on the task, on record. It rings as long as they have the page open (tell the team: keep the tab open during working hours, volume up). If their tab is closed, the ring waits for their next visit — and if you’ve put their email on their Team-tab card, it also lands in their inbox right away, sent from your own Gmail, so their phone still buzzes. At most one email per person every few minutes, and a ring already showing on their screen sends none.

Someone forgot their PIN.

Team tab → their card → Reset PIN. They’re signed out everywhere and you get a fresh PIN to hand them, shown once.

What if a stranger finds our link?

They see the login faces and nothing else — no tasks, no messages, no way in without a PIN. Five wrong guesses locks that person’s login for five minutes. Still, treat the link like the office keys: share it only with the team.

I want to change the tool later.

Paste the newer file over the old one in the editor, then Deploy → Manage deployments → ✏️ → Version: New version → Deploy. The address stays exactly the same — nobody has to re-bookmark anything. If a new version brings new abilities (like sending email), Google asks for permission once more: in the editor pick the function authorizeUpgrade, press Run, and click through Advanced → Allow like on day one — then make the new version.

What does it cost?

Nothing. It runs inside the free allowance of your own Google account, sized far beyond what a small team’s taps and messages will ever use.
